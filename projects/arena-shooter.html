Arena Shooter

A solo first-person arena shooter in Unreal Engine 5, built on a course foundation and extended with my own arena, enemy waves, and weapon work.

Role
Designer & developer (solo)
Engine
Unreal Engine 5, Blueprints
Timeline
Dec. 2025 – Jan. 2026
Focus
Level design, enemy waves, weapons

Ruined Oasis Temple

The assets used to design the temple came from a free Fab asset pack. The arena layout was created as a dynamic space with multiple spawns for enemies, as well as cover layouts corresponding to weapon spawns and behavior.

Level design and enemy behavior

Elevators introduce a vertical element to the game, allowing for different angles and vantage points that trade cover for visibility. Spawn points were placed to ensure initial spawn safety and opportunities for quick weapon upgrades. Enemy AI uses a navmesh bounded by blocking volumes.

Waves, cover, death, and weapon variety

The dynamic enemy wave spawner ensures there is always a constant number of enemies on the field until that spawner runs out, balancing difficulty with fairness. Elevators and ramps are usable by both the player and AI. Weapon spawns are balanced by cover—more powerful weapons are placed in open spots near spawners, while weaker weapons are positioned in safer areas. Map cover was designed around weapon behavior to ensure balanced play.

Overview

Arena Shooter started from a UE5 action-game course and the UE5 First-Person template. I used it as a technical foundation, then designed my own arena and extended the combat systems.

This project served as my foundational introduction to UE5, allowing me to learn the editor, modeling tools, basic AI behavior, materials, and Blueprints without diving into heavy C++ coding right away. It gave me the structure and guidance to ship a finished project while leaving plenty of room for creative expression.

Systems I built

  • Enemy wave system
  • Multiple spawn points
  • Elevator mechanics
  • Cover placement planned around the weapon set

Design decisions

Arena layout and cover placement

Decision
Structured the style, layout, and sightlines of the arena around specific weapon ranges and cover points.
Why
I built a blockout first to test the layout for fairness against enemy behavior. The goal was to create a fast-paced, dynamic experience that prevented issues like spawn deaths or getting rushed by four enemies at once.
Tradeoffs
Building a blockout first and layering environment assets over it significantly increased the time needed to complete the final map.

Spawn points and wave pacing

Decision
Configured spawners to only spawn a new enemy once their previously spawned enemy was defeated.
Why
This prevented the player from getting overrun and allowed individual spawners to be cleared at different times, rewarding tactical and thoughtful play.
Tradeoffs
For balance reasons, I had to keep the spawners static. Given more time, I would like to implement dynamic spawners that relocate or beam enemies down from the sky.

What I built vs. what I adapted

Foundation

  • UE5 action-game YouTube tutorial and UE5 First-Person Shooter template (combat framework and animations)
  • Environment pieces from a Fab asset pack

My own work

  • Arena layout, blockout, and cover placement
  • Multiple spawn points and elevator mechanics
  • Enemy wave system
  • Textures and assets sourced and applied
